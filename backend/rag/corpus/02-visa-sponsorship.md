# Visa Sponsorship

*This document describes policy for Meridian Systems, a fictional company. All processes, permit names, and timeframes are illustrative and do not describe the immigration requirements of any real country.*

## What Meridian sponsors

Meridian sponsors the work authorisation required for an employee to perform their role legally in the destination country. Sponsorship means Meridian acts as the sponsoring entity on the application, pays the government fees, and engages immigration counsel where the destination requires it.

Sponsorship covers the employee's work permit or equivalent, the employer-side filings that permit requires, and the residence permit where the destination issues work and residence authorisation as a combined document. It does not cover the employee's personal travel documents. Employees are responsible for holding a valid passport with sufficient remaining validity, and a passport renewal is not a sponsored cost.

## Sponsorship by destination tier

Where Meridian holds an established entity with in-house immigration support — the Tier 1 destinations — applications are filed directly by the Global Mobility team. Employees interact only with Global Mobility.

In Tier 2 destinations Meridian holds an established entity but engages external immigration counsel to prepare and file. Employees will be contacted directly by the appointed counsel and should expect to correspond with them. Counsel acts on Meridian's instruction; employees should not treat counsel's guidance as personal legal advice.

Tier 3 destinations may be served by a smaller entity or by an employer-of-record partner. The filing route varies by country and is confirmed at the point the assignment is approved. Employees relocating to a Tier 3 destination should expect a longer runway and a heavier document burden, both described in `07-document-requirements.md`.

## Employee permits and dependent permits are separate

This is the most frequent source of confusion in the sponsorship process and warrants stating plainly.

The employee's work permit and a dependent's residence permit are two distinct applications, filed separately, assessed separately, and issued on separate timelines. Approval of the employee permit does not guarantee approval of a dependent permit, and the two are frequently not granted at the same time. In several destinations a dependent application cannot be filed until the employee permit has been issued, which means dependents may follow the employee by several weeks.

Meridian sponsors dependent **residence** permits for a spouse or registered partner and for dependent children, on the same basis as the employee permit. The scope of dependent support, including which relationships qualify, is set out in `09-dependent-and-family-support.md`.

A dependent's right to **work** in the destination is a different matter again. It is not part of the residence permit in most destinations, it is not something Meridian sponsors, and the rules vary considerably. Employees asking whether a spouse or partner may work in the destination must be referred to a Global Mobility adviser rather than given a general answer. This is a mandatory escalation category under `08-escalation-criteria.md`.

## Assignment type and permit class

The permit class depends on how long the employee will be in the destination and what they will do there.

An assignment of under six months will in many destinations be served by a short-stay business visa or a short-term work authorisation rather than a full work permit. These are faster to obtain but carry restrictions: they typically do not permit the employee to be added to a local payroll, and they generally cannot be converted into a longer permit without leaving and re-applying.

An assignment of six months or longer requires a full work permit in every destination Meridian operates in. Employees planning an assignment close to the six-month line should raise this early, because the decision determines the permit class from the outset. Where a short-term assignment is extended past six months, a fresh application is usually required rather than an extension of the existing authorisation, and the employee may need to exit the country while it is processed. `10-repatriation-and-assignment-end.md` covers the extension process.

## Renewals

A permit renewal is not an administrative continuation of the existing permit. In most destinations it is a fresh application, assessed on current criteria, requiring a current set of supporting documents. Documents submitted with the original application are generally not reusable, because destinations impose validity windows on items such as police certificates and civil status documents. `07-document-requirements.md` sets out which documents must be reissued.

Renewal must be initiated no later than ninety days before the existing permit expires. Employees who approach Global Mobility inside that window should be treated as time-critical. Where a permit has already lapsed, or where the ninety-day window cannot be met, the case must be escalated to a Global Mobility adviser immediately; a lapsed permit is not something that can be resolved through the standard renewal route.

## Refusals

Applications are occasionally refused. A refusal is not a matter the standard process can address, and the available options depend on the destination, the refusal ground, and the timing. Any employee reporting a refusal, a request for further evidence that they cannot satisfy, or an appeal must be escalated to a Global Mobility adviser without delay.

## What sponsorship does not include

Meridian does not provide personal immigration advice, does not advise on an employee's route to permanent residence or citizenship, and does not sponsor applications unrelated to the employee's role. Employees seeking advice on their long-term immigration status should be directed to independent counsel at their own cost. The company's ability to answer these questions is limited by design, not by unwillingness.
