# Relocation Timeline and Milestones

*This document describes policy for Meridian Systems, a fictional company. All durations are illustrative and do not reflect real government processing times.*

## Destination tiers

Timelines throughout Meridian's mobility policy are driven by destination tier. The tier reflects the maturity of Meridian's entity in the country and the complexity of the immigration route, not the desirability of the destination.

**Tier 1** — Netherlands, Germany, Ireland, Canada, Australia. Established Meridian entity with in-house immigration support.

**Tier 2** — Singapore, United Arab Emirates, Japan, Switzerland, United Kingdom. Established entity, external immigration counsel engaged for filings.

**Tier 3** — India, Brazil, Poland, Mexico, South Africa. Smaller entity or employer-of-record partner, longest processing, heaviest documentation.

## Overall duration

From assignment approval to the employee's first working day in the destination, employees should plan on approximately **eight to twelve weeks for Tier 1**, **twelve to sixteen weeks for Tier 2**, and **eighteen to twenty-six weeks for Tier 3**.

Tier 3 timelines are roughly double Tier 1 and this difference is not a buffer or a worst case; it is the expected duration. Advising a Tier 3 transferee on a Tier 1 timeline is the most consequential planning error in the process, because employees make irreversible decisions — resigning a lease, giving notice at a school, accepting a start date — against the date they are given.

## Milestones

### 1. Assignment approval

The assignment type, destination, band, and relocating household are confirmed and recorded. This is the point from which every other date is calculated, and it is the point at which entitlements are fixed. An employee's benefits follow from the assignment type recorded here, not from what is discussed informally beforehand.

### 2. Document collection

The employee assembles the documents required for the immigration filing. Requirements vary substantially by tier and are set out in `07-document-requirements.md`.

This stage is the most common cause of delay in the whole process, and the delay is almost always an employee-side one: a document that must be obtained from a home-country authority, apostilled, or translated. Employees relocating to Tier 3 destinations should begin collection immediately on approval rather than waiting to be prompted, because several required items have lead times measured in weeks.

Expect two to three weeks in Tier 1, three to four in Tier 2, and six to ten in Tier 3.

### 3. Immigration filing and processing

Meridian files the application. Processing is out of Meridian's control and cannot be expedited by internal escalation.

Expect four to six weeks in Tier 1, six to ten in Tier 2, and ten to sixteen in Tier 3. Dependent applications, where they can only be filed after the employee permit issues, add a further three to six weeks and are the usual reason a family arrives in stages. `02-visa-sponsorship.md` explains why the two applications are separate.

### 4. Pre-departure

Once the permit is issued, the employee books travel, the moving partner surveys and collects the household shipment, and temporary accommodation is confirmed. Two to three weeks in all tiers.

Employees should not book flights or schedule the pack date before the permit is issued. Doing so is the most common cause of avoidable cost in the process.

### 5. Arrival and registration

The employee arrives and completes in-country registration: local residence registration, tax registration, a bank account, and healthcare enrolment where applicable.

One to two weeks in Tier 1, two to three in Tier 2, and three to five in Tier 3.

### 6. Settling in

The employee finds permanent accommodation and takes delivery of the household shipment. Temporary accommodation is available for thirty days in Tier 1, forty-five in Tier 2, and sixty in Tier 3, per `03-housing-stipend.md`.

## Sequencing dependencies

Several steps cannot be reordered, and understanding why prevents most planning mistakes.

A dependent permit application often cannot be filed until the employee permit has issued, so a family's arrival is gated on the employee's approval rather than running in parallel.

Customs clearance of the household shipment generally requires the employee to hold a residence permit or completed registration in the destination. A shipment can therefore sit at the destination port awaiting a permit, which is why the air freight allowance under `04-shipping-and-moving.md` matters disproportionately.

In several destinations a residential lease cannot be signed without a local bank account, and a bank account cannot be opened without a registered address and an issued permit. This is the circular dependency that drives the longer temporary accommodation periods in Tier 2 and Tier 3.

School enrolment for dependent children is frequently gated on a residence permit and on a registered address, and school admission cycles do not flex to accommodate a delayed permit. Families relocating with school-age children should treat the academic calendar as a fixed constraint and work backwards from it. `09-dependent-and-family-support.md` covers school support.

## Renewals

A permit renewal follows a shorter path than an initial application but is a fresh application rather than a continuation. Renewal must be initiated no later than ninety days before the existing permit expires.

Expect four to eight weeks for a renewal in Tier 1 and Tier 2, and eight to fourteen weeks in Tier 3. Where the ninety-day window has already been missed, or the permit has lapsed, the case is time-critical and must be escalated to a Global Mobility adviser rather than handled through the standard renewal route.

## When timelines slip

Delays in government processing are not escalations; they are expected variance and Global Mobility will keep the employee informed. Escalation is warranted where a delay has created a concrete problem — an expiring permit, a school place at risk, a lease that cannot be signed, or a start date that can no longer be met.
