# Dependent and Family Support

*This document describes policy for Meridian Systems, a fictional company. All figures and provisions are illustrative.*

## Who qualifies as a dependent

For the purposes of Meridian's mobility policy, a dependent is a spouse or registered partner, or a child under eighteen for whom the employee has custody or shared custody. Children aged eighteen to twenty-four qualify where they are in full-time education and financially dependent on the employee.

Meridian's definition is deliberately broad, but the destination country's definition governs what can actually be applied for. Several destinations do not recognise unmarried or unregistered partnerships, and some do not recognise partnerships that Meridian would. Where Meridian's definition and the destination's diverge, the destination's governs the permit application, and Meridian will support the employee in identifying alternative routes where any exist. That identification is case-specific and should be handled by a Global Mobility adviser.

Dependents must be relocating with, or joining, the employee to attract support. A dependent remaining in the home country does not generate an entitlement.

## Dependent residence permits

Meridian sponsors residence permits for qualifying dependents on the same basis as the employee permit, and meets the government fees and counsel costs.

As set out in `02-visa-sponsorship.md`, the dependent application is separate from the employee's and is frequently gated on the employee permit having issued first. Families should plan for the possibility of arriving in stages. Where a dependent application is filed after the employee's, expect a further three to six weeks beyond the employee timeline in `06-relocation-timeline.md`.

Document requirements for dependents, including police certificates for dependents aged sixteen and over in Tier 2 and Tier 3, and consent from a non-relocating parent where applicable, are set out in `07-document-requirements.md`.

## Dependent work authorisation

A dependent's residence permit does not, in most destinations, carry a right to work.

Whether a spouse or partner may work in the destination, what authorisation they would need, and how they would obtain it depends on the destination, on the dependent's own nationality, and on the class of permit the employee holds. Meridian does not sponsor dependent work authorisation and does not advise on it.

Employees asking about a partner's ability to work must be referred to a Global Mobility adviser, who will set out what is known about the destination and, where appropriate, arrange a consultation with immigration counsel. This is a mandatory escalation under `08-escalation-criteria.md` and applies however the question is framed.

The line is worth stating precisely, because the two questions sound alike: **a dependent's right to reside is in scope and answerable; a dependent's right to work is not.**

## School allowance

Meridian contributes toward school fees for dependent children of school age where the employee is on a long-term assignment or a permanent transfer. Short-term assignees do not receive a school allowance, consistent with the treatment of other family benefits under a short-term assignment.

The allowance is paid per child per academic year, directly to the school where possible.

| Destination tier | Annual allowance per child |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 12,000 EUR equivalent |
| Tier 2 | 18,000 EUR equivalent |
| Tier 3 | 15,000 EUR equivalent |

The allowance is intended to cover tuition. It does not cover registration or capital levies charged on admission, uniforms, transport, meals, examination fees, or extracurricular activities. Where fees exceed the allowance, the difference is the employee's cost.

For permanent transferees the allowance runs for the first twenty-four months from the relocation date, aligning with the housing stipend under `03-housing-stipend.md`. For long-term assignees it runs for the duration of the assignment.

Meridian does not select or guarantee a school place. Admission is the family's responsibility, and admission cycles in international schools frequently close months before the academic year and do not flex for a delayed permit. Families with school-age children should treat the academic calendar as the fixed constraint and work the relocation timeline backwards from it.

## Spousal and partner support

Meridian provides a career support service for accompanying spouses and partners on long-term assignments and permanent transfers, delivered through an external provider. The service covers up to twelve hours of consultation and includes CV adaptation to local market conventions, introductions to local recruiters where the provider has them, and guidance on professional credential recognition.

The service is explicitly not a job placement guarantee, and it does not address the question of whether the partner is permitted to work — that remains an escalation as above.

Language tuition is available to the employee and to an accompanying partner where the destination's working or daily language differs from one they already speak, up to forty hours per person, arranged through Meridian's provider.

## Healthcare registration

Meridian enrols the employee and their relocating dependents in the destination healthcare arrangement applicable to the entity, whether that is a statutory scheme, a private policy, or a combination. Enrolment is initiated on arrival and generally requires the residence permit and local registration to be complete first.

Employees should be aware of a gap: the home-country arrangement typically ends on the last day of home employment or residence, while destination enrolment cannot complete until registration is done. Meridian provides interim international coverage for the period between, and employees should confirm it is in place before travelling rather than assuming continuity.

Pre-existing conditions, ongoing treatment, and prescription continuity across a move raise questions the policy cannot answer generically, and employees in that position should speak to a Global Mobility adviser before their departure date is fixed.

## Dependents joining later

Where a dependent joins after the employee — because of a school year, a notice period, or a permit delay — support is unaffected, and the housing and school entitlements apply from the point the dependent arrives.

The relocation allowance, however, is paid once, on the employee's relocation, at the rate applicable to the household as recorded at assignment approval. Where a dependent's relocation was recorded at approval, the higher household figure under `01-relocation-allowance.md` applies even though they arrive later. Where a dependent's relocation was not anticipated at approval and is decided subsequently, adjusting the allowance is a variation and requires an exception.
