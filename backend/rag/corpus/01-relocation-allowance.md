# Relocation Allowance

*This document describes policy for Meridian Systems, a fictional company. All figures are illustrative and do not reflect any real organisation's policy.*

## Purpose and scope

The relocation allowance is a one-time cash payment intended to cover the incidental costs of an international move that are not addressed by any other mobility benefit. It exists because itemised reimbursement of small relocation expenses creates more administrative burden than it saves. Employees are trusted to allocate the allowance as their circumstances require.

This allowance is separate from, and additive to, the housing stipend (see `03-housing-stipend.md`) and the shipping allowance (see `04-shipping-and-moving.md`). Employees sometimes assume these three are a single pooled budget. They are not, and they are approved through different routes.

## Eligibility by assignment type

Entitlement depends first on assignment type, not on band.

Employees on a **permanent transfer** receive the full relocation allowance. Employees on a **long-term assignment** of six to twenty-four months receive the full allowance as well. Employees on a **short-term assignment** of under six months do not receive a relocation allowance at all; their incidental costs are covered through the per-diem arrangement described in `03-housing-stipend.md`.

Where a short-term assignment is later extended beyond six months, the employee becomes eligible for the relocation allowance at the point the extension is formally approved, and the allowance is paid in the following payroll cycle. Extension is not automatic and is not backdated further than the approval date. The mechanics of extension are set out in `10-repatriation-and-assignment-end.md`.

## Standard allowance schedule

The following applies to employees in bands 1 through 4.

| Destination tier | Allowance (single employee) | Allowance (employee with relocating dependents) |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 6,000 EUR equivalent | 9,000 EUR equivalent |
| Tier 2 | 7,500 EUR equivalent | 11,000 EUR equivalent |
| Tier 3 | 9,000 EUR equivalent | 13,500 EUR equivalent |

Tier 3 destinations carry the highest allowance because they typically involve longer processing, more document legalisation cost, and a greater likelihood of an extended temporary accommodation period. Tier assignments are listed in `06-relocation-timeline.md`.

The higher dependent figure applies where one or more dependents relocate with the employee, regardless of the number of dependents. A separate school allowance for dependent children is handled under `09-dependent-and-family-support.md` and is not drawn from this allowance.

## Senior band schedule

Employees in bands 5 and 6 are covered by a distinct schedule rather than a supplement to the standard one. The senior schedule sets the allowance at 14,000 EUR equivalent for a single employee and 19,000 EUR equivalent where dependents relocate, in all destination tiers. Tier does not vary the senior figure, because the senior schedule already assumes the upper end of typical relocation cost.

Advisors and employees should take care not to apply the standard table to a band 5 or band 6 employee. The two schedules are independent, and quoting the standard tier figure to a senior employee understates their entitlement substantially.

## What the allowance may be used for

The allowance is intended for costs such as temporary storage, utility connection and disconnection, replacement of items that cannot be shipped, initial furnishing, local transport in the first weeks, driving licence conversion, and pet relocation. Employees are not required to submit receipts and are not asked to account for how the allowance was spent.

## What the allowance may not be used for

The allowance may not be used to fund immigration application fees, which Meridian pays directly under `02-visa-sponsorship.md`, and it may not be used to fund tax advisory services, which are addressed in `05-tax-equalization.md`. Employees occasionally ask whether they can decline a company-provided service and take the cash equivalent instead. They cannot; the allowance is fixed and is not increased by declining other benefits.

## Payment and tax treatment

The allowance is paid through payroll in the month following the confirmed relocation date, in the currency of the destination payroll. Where the employee's start date in the destination falls after the fifteenth of a month, payment moves to the subsequent cycle.

The allowance is treated as taxable income in most destinations. The specific treatment depends on the destination and on the employee's individual circumstances. **Meridian cannot advise an employee on how the allowance will be taxed in their personal situation.** Employees with questions about their individual tax position must be referred to a Global Mobility adviser, who will arrange access to the company's tax advisory provider. This is a mandatory escalation category and is described in `08-escalation-criteria.md`.

## Requesting a variation

Requests to vary the allowance — whether for an unusual household, an exceptional cost, or a destination not covered by the tier list — are discretionary and cannot be approved through the standard process. All variation requests are handled by Global Mobility on a case-by-case basis and require director-level sign-off. Any request for an exception is a mandatory escalation.
