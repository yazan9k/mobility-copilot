# Repatriation and Assignment End

*This document describes policy for Meridian Systems, a fictional company. All provisions are illustrative.*

## Scope

This document covers what happens when an assignment ends, when it is extended, and when an employee leaves Meridian while on assignment. It applies to assignees; permanent transferees have relocated on local terms and do not repatriate under this policy, though the resignation provisions below apply to them within the periods stated.

## Extending a short-term assignment past six months

A short-term assignment is one of under six months. Extending it beyond six months is not an administrative adjustment to the end date; it changes the assignment type, and with it the employee's entitlements, permit class, and tax position simultaneously.

**Entitlements.** On the approval date of the extension the employee becomes a long-term assignee. The per-diem under `03-housing-stipend.md` ends and the housing stipend begins; the relocation allowance under `01-relocation-allowance.md` becomes payable; and household shipping under `04-shipping-and-moving.md` becomes available. All three take effect prospectively from the approval date. None is backdated, and per-diem already paid is not recovered.

**Permit.** A short-stay business visa or short-term work authorisation generally cannot be converted into a full work permit in place. In most destinations a fresh application is required, and in several the employee must exit the country while it is processed. This is the practical constraint that determines how early an extension must be raised: the permit route, not the internal approval, sets the timeline. `02-visa-sponsorship.md` covers permit classes.

**Tax.** Crossing six months can change the employee's tax residence, and the date from which it changes is not necessarily the extension approval date — it may be the original arrival date, retrospectively. Every extension across the six-month line must be referred to a Global Mobility adviser for tax review. This is a mandatory escalation under `08-escalation-criteria.md` and is not optional even where the employee has no questions.

An extension must be raised with Global Mobility at least twelve weeks before the six-month point in Tier 1 and Tier 2 destinations, and at least sixteen weeks in Tier 3. Extensions raised later can usually still be achieved, but frequently require the employee to leave the destination and re-enter on the new authorisation.

## Planned end of a long-term assignment

Global Mobility contacts the assignee three months before the scheduled end date to confirm the return arrangements and the return date.

Return shipping is covered on the same volume basis as the outbound move under `04-shipping-and-moving.md`. Where goods were held in origin storage during the assignment, storage ends and delivery is arranged to the employee's home-country address. Temporary accommodation in the home country is available for up to fourteen days on return where the employee has no immediate residence available.

Housing stipend and school allowance run to the assignment end date and stop. Where the employee's dependents remain in the destination beyond that date to complete a school year, continuation of the school allowance is a variation and requires an exception.

The employee must complete destination deregistration — local residence registration, tax registration, and healthcare — before departure. Failure to deregister creates ongoing obligations in the destination that can be difficult to unwind later, and in some destinations affects future entry. Global Mobility provides a deregistration checklist, but completion is the employee's responsibility.

Tax equalization support continues through the final tax year in which the employee was covered, and the appointed provider prepares the final returns. The mechanics are set out in `05-tax-equalization.md`; any question about the employee's individual final position escalates.

## Early termination of an assignment

An assignment may end early at the company's initiative, for business reasons, or at the employee's request.

Where the company ends an assignment early, full repatriation support applies as though the assignment had run to term, and the notice period is a minimum of eight weeks.

Where the employee requests an early end, repatriation support is provided at Meridian's discretion. The default position is that return shipping and return travel are covered and the housing stipend runs to the agreed departure date, but this is not an entitlement and the outcome depends on the circumstances. Any request for an early end must be routed to a Global Mobility adviser.

## Resignation during an assignment

Where an employee resigns while on assignment, repatriation support is not provided. Return travel, return shipping, and storage release become the employee's cost, and the housing stipend and school allowance end on the last day of employment.

A permanent transferee who resigns within twelve months of their relocation date is required to repay the relocation allowance in full, and within twenty-four months to repay fifty per cent. The repayment obligation is stated in the assignment letter and is deducted from final pay where local law permits.

The employee's work permit is tied to Meridian's sponsorship and generally ceases to be valid on the last day of employment, frequently with a short grace period for the employee to leave or find alternative sponsorship. The length of that period varies substantially by destination. An employee who resigns while holding a sponsored permit must be escalated to a Global Mobility adviser, because the consequences for their right to remain are immediate and destination-specific.

Where dependents hold permits derived from the employee's, those permits are affected in the same way and on the same timeline.

## Assignment ending in a different destination

Where an assignment ends because the employee is moving to a further assignment in a third country, this document does not apply. The move is treated as a new relocation from the current destination, and entitlements are set fresh at the new assignment's approval under `06-relocation-timeline.md`. Origin for the purposes of shipping and travel is the current destination, not the employee's original home country.

## Repatriation and re-entry

Meridian does not guarantee a specific role on repatriation. The assignment letter states whether a return role is held open and on what terms, and where it does not, the employee is supported through the internal recruitment process during the final three months of the assignment.

Questions about what role an employee will return to are a matter for their manager and HR business partner rather than Global Mobility, and should be redirected accordingly rather than answered from mobility policy.
