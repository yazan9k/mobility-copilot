# Document Requirements

*This document describes policy for Meridian Systems, a fictional company. Document lists and validity windows are illustrative and do not describe any real country's immigration requirements.*

## How to use this document

Requirements vary by destination tier, by whether the application is an initial one or a renewal, and by whether dependents are included. The exact list for a given case is confirmed by Global Mobility or by appointed counsel at the point of filing. This document sets out the baseline so employees can begin collection early, which is the single most effective thing an employee can do to protect their timeline.

## Core documents — all destinations, initial application

Every initial application requires: a passport valid for at least six months beyond the intended permit end date, with at least two blank pages; passport-standard photographs meeting the destination's specification; a signed employment contract or assignment letter issued by the destination entity; evidence of the employee's qualifications where the role requires them; and a completed application form supplied by Global Mobility or counsel.

Employees should check passport validity at the point of assignment approval rather than later. A passport renewal is not a sponsored cost under `02-visa-sponsorship.md` and can add several weeks.

## Additional documents by tier

**Tier 1 destinations** generally require only the core set, plus proof of address in the home country and, where the role is regulated, verification of the qualifying credential.

**Tier 2 destinations** additionally require a police certificate or certificate of good conduct from every country the employee has resided in for six months or more in the preceding five years, and educational certificates verified or attested by the issuing institution.

**Tier 3 destinations** require everything above, plus a medical examination completed at a panel clinic designated by the destination authority, a birth certificate, and — where the employee is married or relocating with dependents — a marriage certificate and each child's birth certificate. Tier 3 filings also frequently require documents to be legalised rather than apostilled, which is a slower and more involved process.

## Validity windows

Several documents are only accepted if recently issued, and this is what makes document collection a live task rather than a filing exercise.

Police certificates are generally accepted only if issued within the preceding three months at the point of filing, or six months in some Tier 1 destinations. Medical examinations are typically valid for three months. Civil status extracts — birth and marriage certificates — are frequently required to be recent extracts rather than the original historical document, with acceptance windows of three to six months depending on destination.

The practical consequence is that these documents cannot be obtained far in advance and held. Obtaining a police certificate the moment an assignment is approved, when the filing is fourteen weeks away in a Tier 3 destination, will produce a document that has expired by the time it is needed.

## Apostille, legalisation, and translation

Documents issued by an authority in one country generally require authentication before another country's authority will accept them.

Where both countries are party to the relevant convention, an **apostille** from the issuing country's designated authority is sufficient. Turnaround is typically one to three weeks.

Where they are not, the document requires **legalisation**, a chain of certifications passing through the issuing country's foreign ministry and then the destination country's diplomatic mission. Turnaround is typically three to eight weeks and is the longest single lead time in the document set. Several Tier 3 destinations require legalisation.

Translation into the destination language is required where the destination specifies it, and must generally be a certified or sworn translation rather than an informal one. Translation is arranged and paid for by Meridian through appointed counsel; employees should not commission their own translations, as they are frequently rejected.

## Dependent documents

Where dependents relocate, each dependent requires their own passport meeting the same validity rule, photographs, and evidence of the relationship to the employee — a marriage certificate for a spouse or registered partner, a birth certificate for each child.

Tier 2 and Tier 3 destinations additionally require a police certificate for each dependent aged sixteen or over, and Tier 3 requires a medical examination for each dependent. Where a child's parents are separated or one parent is not relocating, destinations frequently require documented consent from the non-relocating parent, and this requirement should be identified early because it can be difficult to satisfy at short notice.

The scope of who qualifies as a dependent is set out in `09-dependent-and-family-support.md`.

## Renewals

A renewal is not a continuation of the existing application and does not reuse the documents filed with it.

Employees frequently assume that because Meridian and the destination authority already hold their birth certificate, marriage certificate, and police certificate, a renewal is a matter of confirming that nothing has changed. It is not. A renewal is assessed as a fresh application, and every document subject to a validity window must be reissued. In practice this means a new police certificate, a new medical examination where the destination requires one, and fresh civil status extracts.

Documents not subject to a validity window — educational certificates, for instance — can usually be reused, and Global Mobility will confirm which items carry over.

What a renewal adds beyond the initial set is evidence of the period already spent in the destination: proof of continuous employment, evidence of the registered address, and in some destinations proof of tax compliance or social security contributions for the period held.

Because reissued documents carry the same lead times as they did originally, and because legalisation may be required again, renewal document collection must begin at the point the ninety-day renewal window opens rather than when the permit is close to expiry. `06-relocation-timeline.md` sets out renewal durations by tier.

## Where a document cannot be obtained

Employees occasionally cannot obtain a required document — a police certificate from a country that will not issue to non-residents, a birth certificate that was never registered, or a document lost with no reissuing authority available. Destinations sometimes accept a sworn affidavit or alternative evidence in these situations, but acceptance is discretionary and case-specific.

An employee reporting that they cannot obtain a required document should be escalated to a Global Mobility adviser rather than advised on a substitute. The same applies where a document reveals something the employee is concerned about, such as a criminal record disclosure; that is a matter for an adviser and, where appropriate, counsel.
