# Tax Equalization

*This document describes policy for Meridian Systems, a fictional company. Nothing in this document is tax advice, and the arrangements described are illustrative.*

## The boundary this document sits on

This document explains what Meridian's tax equalization policy is and who it covers. It deliberately does not explain what any individual employee's tax position will be, because that depends on facts Meridian does not hold and on rules that vary by jurisdiction and by year.

Every question of the form *"what will I actually pay"*, *"how will my equity be treated"*, or *"should I do X before I move"* is an individual tax question. Those must be routed to a Global Mobility adviser, who will arrange access to Meridian's appointed tax advisory provider. This is a mandatory escalation category under `08-escalation-criteria.md`, and it holds regardless of how straightforward the question appears or how confidently it is asked.

## What tax equalization means

Tax equalization is a mechanism intended to make an employee's tax burden neutral with respect to their relocation. The principle is that an employee should be no better and no worse off in tax terms for having accepted an international assignment than they would have been had they remained in their home location.

In practice this is implemented through a hypothetical tax deduction. Meridian calculates what the employee's home-country tax would have been on their Meridian compensation had they not relocated, and deducts that hypothetical amount through payroll. Meridian then meets the employee's actual tax liability in the home and host locations on that compensation. Where the actual liability is higher than the hypothetical, Meridian absorbs the difference; where it is lower, Meridian retains the benefit.

The employee's take-home outcome is therefore intended to track what it would have been at home, rather than tracking the destination's tax regime.

## Who is covered

Tax equalization applies to employees on a **long-term assignment** of six to twenty-four months and to employees on a **permanent transfer**, in the latter case for the first twenty-four months from the relocation date.

It does not apply to employees on a **short-term assignment** of under six months. Short-term assignees generally remain tax-resident at home and remain on home payroll, and the per-diem arrangement described in `03-housing-stipend.md` is structured with that in mind. Where a short-term assignment is extended past six months the employee's tax position may change materially and from a date that is not necessarily the extension approval date. Extensions across the six-month line are an escalation in every case; see `10-repatriation-and-assignment-end.md`.

## The twenty-four month limit

For permanent transferees, equalization support ends twenty-four months after the relocation date. From that point the employee is treated as a local employee of the destination entity for tax purposes and bears their own tax position on local terms.

Employees are notified six months before support ends, and are offered a final consultation with the tax advisory provider to plan for the transition. Employees approaching this boundary frequently have questions about what changes for them specifically. Those are individual tax questions and escalate.

## What Meridian covers in advisory services

Meridian pays for the following through its appointed provider: an arrival consultation before or shortly after the move; preparation of home and host country tax returns for each year in which the employee is covered by equalization; and the final transition consultation where equalization is ending.

Meridian does not pay for advice on the employee's personal investments, property, inheritance, or any matter unconnected to their Meridian compensation. It does not pay for representation in a tax dispute or audit arising from the employee's personal affairs. Employees may engage the provider privately for these matters at their own cost.

## Compensation elements that complicate the position

Certain compensation elements interact with a relocation in ways that are not reducible to a general rule, and employees holding them should be routed to the advisory provider early rather than late.

Equity awards vesting across a move date are the most common. Where an award was granted in one jurisdiction and vests while the employee is resident in another, the taxing rights over that award may be apportioned between jurisdictions on a basis that depends on both countries' rules and on the treaty between them. Meridian's payroll will operate whatever withholding the relevant authorities require, but the outcome for the individual is not something this policy can state.

Bonuses paid after a move but earned before it, pension contributions, and any income the employee receives from outside Meridian raise similar questions. In all cases the answer is a referral, not an explanation.

## Social security

Social security is administered separately from income tax and is not covered by the equalization mechanism. Where a treaty or totalisation agreement exists between the home and host countries, Meridian will apply for a certificate of coverage so that the employee remains in their home scheme where that is available and advantageous. Where no agreement exists, the employee will typically enter the host country scheme.

The practical consequences for an individual's contribution record and future entitlements vary and are, again, a matter for the advisory provider.

## Employee obligations

Employees covered by equalization must provide the advisory provider with the information it requests, on time, and must file any return the provider prepares. Failure to do so can create a liability that Meridian will not meet on the employee's behalf.

Employees must also notify Global Mobility of changes that bear on residence: a change in the number of days spent in each country, a change in family circumstances, or the acquisition of property in either location. Employees should notify rather than self-assess whether the change matters.
