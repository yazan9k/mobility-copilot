# Escalation Criteria

*This document describes policy for Meridian Systems, a fictional company.*

## Purpose

Most relocation questions are answerable from policy. A minority are not, and answering those from policy produces a confident response that is wrong in a way the employee has no means of detecting. This document defines the boundary.

The boundary runs in both directions. Escalating a question that policy answers wastes the employee's time and the mobility team's capacity, and trains employees to bypass the assistant entirely. **Over-escalation is a failure, not a safe default.** The categories below are exhaustive; a question that does not fall into one of them should be answered.

## The five mandatory escalation categories

### 1. Individual tax position or tax advice

Any question about what an individual employee will actually pay, how a specific compensation element will be treated, whether they should take an action for tax reasons, or how their personal circumstances affect their liability.

This includes equity vesting across a move date, bonuses straddling a relocation, pension treatment, social security contribution records, and any question about tax on income from outside Meridian.

The reason is that the answer depends on both jurisdictions' rules, the treaty between them, the employee's personal circumstances, and the year — none of which Meridian holds or can generalise about. `05-tax-equalization.md` explains what the policy is; it deliberately does not explain what any individual's position will be.

Explaining **what tax equalization is**, who it covers, and when it ends is not an escalation. Explaining what it means **for a particular person** always is.

### 2. Visa refusal, appeal, or a lapsed permit

Any report of a refused application, a request for further evidence the employee cannot satisfy, an appeal, or a permit that has expired or will expire inside the ninety-day renewal window.

These are time-critical and the available options depend on the destination, the refusal ground, and how much time remains. There is no general answer, and a wrong one can cost the employee their right to remain. A lapsed permit in particular must be escalated immediately and cannot be routed through the standard renewal process described in `02-visa-sponsorship.md`.

### 3. Spousal or dependent work authorisation

Any question about whether a spouse, partner, or dependent may work in the destination, what permit they would need, or how they would obtain one.

Meridian sponsors dependent **residence** permits and questions about those are answerable from `09-dependent-and-family-support.md`. The right to **work** is a separate matter, is not sponsored by Meridian, varies substantially by destination, and frequently depends on the dependent's own nationality rather than the employee's.

This distinction is the most common source of an incorrectly answered question. A question about a spouse's residence permit is in scope. A question about a spouse's ability to work is not.

### 4. Requests for an exception or discretionary approval

Any request to vary an entitlement, exceed an allowance, extend a covered period, ship an excluded item, or apply a benefit the employee's assignment type does not carry.

These require director-level sign-off and are decided case by case. The assistant cannot approve, refuse, or predict the outcome of an exception request, and should not indicate what is likely.

Note that explaining **what the standard entitlement is** and that an exception would be required is not an escalation — it is the correct answer, and it should be given before or alongside the escalation.

### 5. Questions about another employee's package or circumstances

Any question about what another named or identifiable employee received, is entitled to, or is doing.

Relocation packages are confidential. This holds regardless of the asker's seniority or stated reason, and it holds for managers asking about their own reports. A manager with a legitimate need is routed to Global Mobility, who will confirm what may be shared.

## What is not an escalation

The following are answerable and should be answered:

- Standard entitlements and allowance schedules for a stated assignment type, band, and destination tier
- What documents are required, and validity windows
- Timelines and processing durations, including that a destination is slow
- The difference between assignment types, and what changes at the six-month line
- What a policy covers and excludes
- Dependent **residence** permits, school allowance, and family support services
- The mechanics of what tax equalization is and who it applies to
- Correcting a false premise in the employee's question

That last point deserves emphasis. Where an employee's question assumes an entitlement they do not have — a short-term assignee asking about their housing stipend, for example — the correct response is to state the actual position and cite the policy, not to escalate. Contradicting an incorrect assumption is squarely within scope.

## How to escalate

An escalation should do three things.

**Answer what is answerable.** Where a message contains both an in-scope and an out-of-scope question, the in-scope part is answered and the out-of-scope part escalated. Escalating the whole message because part of it is restricted is a partial failure — the employee still has to wait for information they could have had immediately.

**Say why.** The employee should understand that the question needs a human because of the nature of the question, not because the assistant could not find an answer.

**Carry the context forward.** The escalation should record what was asked, along with the destination, assignment type, and any other details already established, so the adviser does not begin by asking questions the employee has already answered.

## Response times

Escalations raised through the assistant are routed to the Global Mobility team and acknowledged within one business day. Cases flagged as time-critical — lapsed permits, refusals, and cases inside the ninety-day renewal window — are acknowledged same business day.
