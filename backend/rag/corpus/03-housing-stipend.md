# Housing Stipend and Temporary Accommodation

*This document describes policy for Meridian Systems, a fictional company. All figures are illustrative.*

## Overview

Meridian supports relocating employees with accommodation in two distinct phases: a period of company-arranged temporary accommodation on arrival, followed for eligible employees by an ongoing monthly housing stipend. The two are governed by different rules and it is common for employees to assume entitlement to the second because they have received the first.

## Temporary accommodation on arrival

All relocating employees, including short-term assignees, receive company-arranged temporary accommodation from their arrival date. The purpose is to give the employee a base from which to find permanent housing without pressure.

The standard period is thirty days in Tier 1 destinations, forty-five days in Tier 2, and sixty days in Tier 3. The longer periods reflect the greater difficulty of securing a lease in markets where a local bank account, a registered address, or an issued residence permit is a precondition of signing — a sequencing problem covered in `06-relocation-timeline.md`.

Accommodation is booked by Meridian's relocation partner. Employees may express preferences on location and size but do not select the property directly. Extensions beyond the standard period are possible where a permit delay has genuinely blocked a lease signing, and are approved by Global Mobility on evidence. An extension requested for reasons of preference rather than blockage is a discretionary exception and must be escalated.

## Ongoing housing stipend

The monthly housing stipend is a contribution toward the employee's accommodation cost in the destination. It is paid through payroll and does not require the employee to submit rent receipts, though Meridian may ask for proof of a tenancy at the point the stipend commences.

Eligibility is determined by assignment type. Employees on a **permanent transfer** and employees on a **long-term assignment** of six to twenty-four months receive the housing stipend. Employees on a **short-term assignment** of under six months do not. This is the single most misunderstood point in the accommodation policy, and it is worth being explicit: a short-term assignee is not entitled to the housing stipend at any band and in any destination.

## Stipend schedule

The following monthly figures apply to employees in bands 1 through 4.

| Destination tier | Single employee | Employee with relocating dependents |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 1,400 EUR equivalent | 2,100 EUR equivalent |
| Tier 2 | 1,800 EUR equivalent | 2,700 EUR equivalent |
| Tier 3 | 1,100 EUR equivalent | 1,650 EUR equivalent |

Tier 2 carries the highest figure because it includes the destinations with the most expensive residential markets. Tier 3 is lowest for the same reason inverted, notwithstanding that Tier 3 carries the highest relocation allowance under `01-relocation-allowance.md`; the two schedules respond to different cost drivers and do not move together.

Employees in bands 5 and 6 receive the tier figure plus a further 40 per cent.

## Stipend duration

For a long-term assignment the stipend runs for the duration of the assignment. For a permanent transfer the stipend runs for twenty-four months from the relocation date and then ends, on the reasoning that a permanent transferee has by then established themselves on local terms. Employees are notified three months before the stipend ends.

The stipend continues uninterrupted during a permit renewal, provided the employee remains in the destination and the renewal is filed within the required window. A renewal does not restart the twenty-four month clock.

## Per-diem for short-term assignees

Employees on a short-term assignment of under six months receive a daily per-diem in place of both the relocation allowance and the housing stipend. The per-diem is intended to cover accommodation beyond the temporary period, meals, and incidental local costs.

Rates are 95 EUR equivalent per day in Tier 1, 120 EUR equivalent in Tier 2, and 80 EUR equivalent in Tier 3. The per-diem is paid monthly in arrears and is not payable for days the employee spends outside the destination country.

Short-term assignees sometimes ask whether they may forgo the per-diem in favour of the housing stipend, on the basis that a stipend would be worth more over the assignment. They may not. The per-diem and the stipend attach to assignment type and are not interchangeable. Where an assignment is extended past six months the employee moves onto the stipend prospectively from the approval date of the extension; the change is not backdated, and the per-diem already paid is not recovered. `10-repatriation-and-assignment-end.md` sets out the extension process.

## Deposits and agency fees

Where a destination market requires a rental deposit, Meridian will advance the deposit against repayment at the end of the tenancy, up to a maximum of three months' rent. Agency and broker fees, where customary in the destination, are reimbursed on receipt up to one month's rent. Both are available to stipend-eligible employees only; short-term assignees in temporary accommodation do not incur them.

## What is not covered

The stipend does not cover utilities, internet, contents insurance, parking, or local property taxes, all of which remain the employee's cost. It does not cover accommodation for visitors, and it does not increase where an employee chooses accommodation above the stipend value. Employees are free to spend above the stipend; the difference is theirs.

Requests to increase the stipend for individual circumstances are discretionary and must be escalated to a Global Mobility adviser rather than answered from this policy.
